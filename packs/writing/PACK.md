# Writing pack

Loaded on demand. Universal rules live in the kernel core and are not repeated here.

## Evidence that matters

The brief, the audience, and the existing voice. Before revising anything, read
enough of the surrounding text to know how it already sounds; a revision that is
better in isolation and wrong in register is a worse revision.

What the piece is for outranks what it says. Copy that reads beautifully and does
not move the reader is decoration.

## Execution patterns

**direct** — a bounded revision: copy edit, tighten, fix tone in a paragraph. One
pass. Return the revised text, not a report about it.

**gated** — a substantive draft or restructure. Agree the angle, audience, and
length first, write, then read it back against the brief.

**trajectory** — voice-finding work where the target is only recognizable once
seen. Draft, react, redraft. Stop when two passes produce no improvement, or when
the remaining changes are preference rather than quality.

## Verification

Read the revised text against the brief. For tone and voice changes where taste is
the criterion, use blinded pairwise comparison against the prior version rather
than self-assessment: the author of a revision is the worst judge of it.

Preserve meaning unless changing it was the request. Check that every factual
claim in the original survived the edit intact.

Show the revision, not a description of it. "I tightened the second paragraph" is
not a deliverable.

## Hazards

- **Voice flattening.** Generic competent prose is the default failure. Specific
  beats polished.
- **Silent meaning drift.** Tightening removes qualifiers that were load-bearing.
- **Over-revision.** Asked to fix grammar, do not restructure the argument.
- **Fabricated specifics.** Never invent a statistic, quote, testimonial, or
  credential to make copy land.
- **Em dashes and AI tells.** Match the existing text's punctuation habits.
